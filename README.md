Abstract

This study examines changes in Finland’s annual carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions from 2010 to 2025 using country-level data published by Our World in Data (OWID). The analysis focuses on territorial CO2 emissions from fossil fuels and industrial processes, excluding land-use change, and also examines emissions per capita. The data are filtered for Finland using the ISO-3 code FIN. Descriptive statistics, annual percentage changes, graphical trend analysis, and an exploratory linear regression are used to describe the observed pattern. The numerical results are generated by the accompanying R script from the latest available OWID data and are saved in the repository’s results/ and figures/ folders. Because the dataset is periodically revised, the retrieval date and downloaded data snapshot should be retained when the analysis is submitted.

Keywords: carbon dioxide, CO2 emissions, Finland, territorial emissions, per-capita emissions, RStudio, time-series analysis

1. Research Question

How have annual CO2 emissions and CO2 emissions per capita changed in Finland from 2010 to 2025?

2. Data Source and Description

This analysis uses two indicators from Our World in Data: annual CO2 emissions and annual CO2 emissions per capita. The annual total indicator measures carbon dioxide emissions from fossil fuels and industrial processes, excluding land-use change. The per-capita indicator expresses annual territorial CO2 emissions per person.

Finland is selected using its ISO-3 country code, FIN. The study period is 2010-2025, with a maximum of 16 annual observations. The annual total series is originally expressed in tonnes and is converted to million tonnes (Mt) by dividing by 1,000,000. Per-capita emissions are expressed in tonnes per person.

The indicators represent territorial emissions. They assign emissions to the country where production occurs, do not adjust for emissions embodied in imported goods, and exclude international aviation and shipping bunker emissions from individual country totals. The data source and its methodology should therefore be considered when interpreting the results.

3. Methodology

The data are retrieved directly from the OWID Grapher CSV endpoints using R. The script filters observations for Finland (FIN) and the years 2010-2025, merges annual and per-capita emissions by year, and checks for missing observations.

The following analyses are performed:

1. Descriptive statistics: first and last year, first- and last-year emissions, overall percentage change, peak emissions year and value, minimum emissions year and value, latest per-capita emissions, mean, and standard deviation.
2. Annual percentage change: calculated as (current-year emissions / previous-year emissions - 1) x 100.
3. Graphical analysis: line graphs are produced for annual CO2 emissions, per-capita CO2 emissions, and annual percentage change.
4. Exploratory trend analysis: a simple ordinary least-squares regression is fitted with annual emissions (Mt) as the response and calendar year as the explanatory variable. The slope, R-squared, and p-value are reported as descriptive measures of the linear time trend. The regression does not establish causation.

All data processing, calculations, tables, and figures are reproducible using the R script included in this repository.

4. How to Reproduce the Analysis

1. Install R, RStudio, and Git.

6. Interpretation and Discussion

The analysis should be interpreted by considering both the direction of the long-term trend and the year-to-year fluctuations. The first- and last-year values and the overall percentage change describe the net change across the study period. The peak and minimum values identify the years with the highest and lowest recorded emissions in the selected interval. Per-capita emissions provide additional context by expressing emissions relative to population size.

The annual percentage-change graph identifies years in which emissions increased or decreased compared with the preceding year. A negative value represents a year-on-year decline, while a positive value represents an increase. The exploratory regression summarizes the average linear direction of change over the period; it should not be interpreted as proof that emissions changed at a constant rate.

Potential explanations such as changes in energy production, fossil-fuel use, industrial activity, weather, economic conditions, and electricity imports may be discussed as contextual factors only when supported by appropriate external references. These factors are not directly tested by the descriptive analysis and must not be presented as causal findings of this study.

7. Limitations

• The analysis focuses on CO2 emissions from fossil fuels and industrial processes and does not represent all greenhouse gases.
• The selected indicator excludes land-use change and forestry emissions.
• Territorial accounting does not include the full emissions footprint of imported goods consumed in Finland.
• International aviation and shipping bunker emissions are not allocated to individual country totals in this indicator.
• OWID and its underlying data providers may revise historical estimates; results can therefore differ depending on the retrieval date.
• The 2025 value may be an estimate and should be interpreted according to the dataset’s documentation.
• The study is descriptive. The regression identifies an association with calendar time and does not establish the causes of emissions changes.
• The short study period contains at most 16 annual observations, which limits the strength of more complex statistical inference.
